What is the LM head?

The last layer of a language model, explained from zero.

A language model does exactly one thing: guess the next word.
The LM head is the part that makes the guess. Everything before it just prepares for that moment.

1. A language model has two parts

Think of the model as a reader followed by a guesser:

input
"The capital of France is"
the text so far
→
part 1 · the reader
Transformer layers
28 layers read the text and build up understanding
→
what comes out
One vector
1,536 numbers summarizing "what should come next"
→
part 2 · the guesser
LM head
turns that vector into a score for every word it knows
→
output
"Paris" 🏆
highest-scoring word wins

The reader's output — that vector of 1,536 numbers — is the model's internal "thought." It isn't a word yet. It's a point in a huge abstract space that means something like "a French capital city should come next." Somebody has to translate that thought into an actual word. That translator is the LM head.

2. The LM head is just a giant scorecard

Here's the whole trick. The model's vocabulary has ~152,000 words (tokens). The LM head stores one row of 1,536 numbers for every word — you can think of each row as that word's "direction" or fingerprint in the same space the thought lives in.

To make its guess, the LM head compares the thought against every row, one by one. Each comparison is a dot product: multiply the numbers pairwise and add them up. If the thought and a word's row point the same way, the score is high. That's it — no magic, just 152,000 similarity checks:

the thought
(1,536 numbers)
·
⋮   151,930 more rows   ⋮
the LM head: one row per word
=
⋮
one score per word
("logits")

So when people say the LM head is "a linear layer," this is all they mean: one matrix multiplication — thought vector × word matrix → 152,000 scores. The scores are called logits; a softmax then squashes them into probabilities.

3. Try it yourself

Below is a miniature LM head with a 4-number thought and 5 words in its vocabulary. The four sliders are the thought vector. Each candidate word has its fixed row of 4 numbers (shown on hover). Drag the sliders and watch the dot products — and the winning word — change.

"The capital of France is Paris"
Real models work the same way, except the thought has 1,536 dimensions instead of 4 — and they aren't neatly labeled like "France-ness"; the model invents its own dimensions during training. Try dragging food-ness up: the same sentence starts predicting croissant. That's literally how sampling weirdness happens.

4. Why it matters: this thing is huge

For the model in this project (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B), the LM head is one matrix of 1,536 × 151,936 numbers:

~233M
parameters in this single layer — a sizable slice of a "1.5B" model
151,936
scores produced per token position — one for every word it knows
~0.3 MB
memory for one position's scores (fp16). Sounds small…
~3 GB
…but score a 10,000-token trace at every position and it's gigabytes

During generation that cost is unavoidable — the model must score every word to pick the next one, for every single token it writes.

5. And why we sometimes skip it

Our steering-vector extraction feeds an already-finished reasoning trace back through the model just to record the layer-20 "thoughts." There is no next word to guess — so running the guesser would compute those ~3 GB of scores and immediately throw them away.

That's what this one line in hidden_analysis.py does:

base_model = getattr(model, "model", model) — "give me just the reader, without the guesser." Same thoughts out, none of the wasted scores. (The full story of that line is in the PR #5 conflict page.)

One-line summary: the LM head is the model's final translator — a single giant multiplication that turns the internal "thought" vector into a score for every word in the vocabulary, so the highest-scoring word can be written next.
